SC8280XP) on a **mainline Linux kernel**, with hardware Vulkan on the Adreno 690.

than tuned by feel, and the driver can now show its own work.

## Touch

If you have been on v0.6.1 you may have noticed lists that would not fling,
gestures that cancelled half-way, and "ghost" taps appearing along a fast
swipe. Those were one bug, and it was ours.

**Fast swipes no longer break up.** The touch preset we shipped enabled the
driver's jump detection with a threshold that, because the test compared raw
displacement rather than deviation from the predicted position, acted as a
1.0 m/s speed limit. Above it the driver reported no contact at all, and a
single flick arrived at Android as a dozen separate touches — which is exactly
what a cancelled fling, a dropped gesture and a phantom tap look like. Measured
on the device: the same 8 seconds of flicking produced 87 touches before and 6
after. The test now compares against the predicted position, so it catches a
real finger swap at any speed and never fires on a smooth slide
([#114](https://github.com/vahiru/gaokun-android/blob/main/docs/stage4-findings.md)).

**Slow drags track the finger.** The driver hard-coded an input `fuzz` of 8 —
a value chosen so that libinput on desktop Linux would not read a resting
finger as a swipe. On Android that dead band was discarding every movement
under 0.4 mm and damping everything under 1.6 mm. We measured what it was
protecting against: with the finger down, perturbing the real capacitance frame
by the real idle noise moves the reported centroid by about 0.15 output units —
less than half of one unit, below the output quantum. `fuzz` is now 0 (set in
the device tree, so it survives driver updates). On slow drags, the share of
frames where the finger moved but the pointer did not fell from 13.7% to 1.8%
([#116](https://github.com/vahiru/gaokun-android/blob/main/docs/stage4-findings.md)).

**Press latency 25 → 17 ms.** The two-frame debounce on a new contact was
insurance against noise; at idle the panel shows zero pixels anywhere near the
detection threshold (11× headroom), so one frame is enough. Verified with 36
taps and swipes: no spurious short contacts.

**Contact size and pressure are reported.** The driver previously registered
no `ABS_MT_TOUCH_MAJOR` / `ABS_MT_PRESSURE` axes, so the framework had nothing
to do palm rejection with. The axes are now enabled and report real values
(the driver's default of reporting constants when the axes exist has been
fixed — every contact used to look like a needle point). This needed a second
fix on the way out: the OTA post-install script never propagated kernel
command-line changes from `boot.img` into the boot entry, so the first build
of this release came up without the axes. It does now; updates from v0.6.1
get the new command line.

**Known:** a palm resting on the panel fragments into several contacts rather
than being rejected as one. Taps with the other hand still work. Three
threshold-based fixes were tried and measured; all three either did not
separate palm from fingertip or, in one case, broke fast swipes again (a
moving finger has a lower capacitance peak than a resting one). Left for a
later release; the counters below are what will make it tractable.

## The touch driver can now show its work

Six driver defects fixed
([#115](https://github.com/vahiru/gaokun-android/blob/main/docs/stage4-findings.md)):
the connected-region table stopped scanning the rest of the panel when full
(noisy environments could drop real fingers on the lower half of the screen);
palm zones were deleted rather than marked; the SPI read retry re-sent the
previous reply instead of the command; each frame was fetched in two transfers
instead of one; the axis resolution was reported as unknown; and the driver
now builds warning-free under `W=1`.

New diagnostics (no behaviour change): per-stage counters under
`/sys/bus/spi/devices/spi0.0/algo/stats`, a log of the last 16 contact births
(`contacts_log`), and the raw and processed 40×60 capacitance frames under
`/sys/kernel/debug/himax-hx83121a/`. Tooling to read them is in
`scripts/touch/`. One direct result: the panel firmware does track a per-pixel
baseline (idle frame within −143…+69 counts of the driver's constant baseline),
which had been an open question.

## Known issues

* SELinux is still `permissive`.
* Audio (and Bluetooth) can deadlock after long uptime; a watchdog records the
  evidence under `/data/vendor/gaokun3/hangdump-*` — please attach it if you hit
  this.
* Internet downloads on the device are slow (1–2 MB/s) for reasons not yet
  established; LAN throughput is fine. In-system OTA is affected.
* Rear camera: no tuning file yet; dim scenes are dark, flash shots overexpose.

## For developers

Kernel patches `0037`–`0047` in `patches/` (touch), `#114`–`#116` in
`docs/stage4-findings.md`. The touch tooling in `scripts/touch/` is
documented in its README, including the rule that any change to a detection
threshold must be validated with fast swipes, not taps.
